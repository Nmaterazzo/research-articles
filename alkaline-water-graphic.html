Alkaline Water:
Not a Scam,
Just Misunderstood

Everyone online told you alkaline water is snake oil because your stomach acid neutralizes it immediately. That answer is half-right. The buffering system doing the neutralizing has a real cost, and for specific populations, reducing that cost has measurable benefits backed by clinical trials. Here is who actually benefits and why.

High-intensity anaerobic athletes

The real problem: Alkaline water is sold to everyone for cancer prevention, detox, and longevity. That evidence does not exist. But dismissing every application because the marketing is bad ignores real RCT data in athletes and GERD patients. The truth is audience-specific.
01
The Buffering Argument: Who Is Right and Who Is Missing the Point
StatPearls acid-base physiology · BJA Education acid-base review 2022 · AK water acid-base balance RCT · pH range data

The dismissal goes like this: your blood pH is locked at 7.35-7.45, your stomach acid neutralizes alkaline water immediately, and therefore it does nothing. That first part is true. But the argument conflates "your blood pH does not change" with "nothing is happening," which ignores the cost of maintaining that tight range.

The pH Scale and Why Blood Range Is Non-Negotiable
Normal blood pH 7.35-7.45 · stomach pH 1.5-3.5 · urine pH 4.5-8.0 (variable) · alkaline water pH 7.5-9.5
Blood pH is the most tightly regulated variable in human physiology. Urine pH, by contrast, varies widely and reflects how hard the kidneys are working to offload acid. When you eat an acid-producing diet or exercise intensely, urine pH drops as the kidneys excrete more hydrogen ions. Alkaline water reduces that excretion load.
How the Buffering System Maintains Blood pH
StatPearls physiology 2022 · BJA Education acid-base review 2022 · three-system model: chemical buffers, lungs, kidneys
The Nuance the Influencers Skip The critics are right that drinking alkaline water does not meaningfully change your blood pH. That is settled physiology. What they miss is that the buffering system maintaining that pH is not free. During high-intensity anaerobic exercise, the kidneys and chemical buffers are working at maximum capacity to prevent acidosis. Reducing the incoming acid load — even modestly — gives those systems more headroom. That is not snake oil. That is basic buffer chemistry applied to sports physiology.
02
The Baking Soda Bridge: The Proof of Concept Everyone Ignores
Grgic et al. ISSN Position Stand · J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2021 · 18:61 · meta-analysis of 150+ studies

Alkaline water is essentially a low-concentration version of sodium bicarbonate supplementation. If you call alkaline water snake oil but accept that elite cyclists take baking soda before events, you have an inconsistency to explain. The 2021 ISSN position stand on sodium bicarbonate is the best published framework for understanding why the mechanism is real.

Sodium Bicarbonate and Exercise Performance: ISSN Position Stand Data
Grgic et al. ISSN Position Stand 2021 · J Int Soc Sports Nutr 18:61 · meta-analysis of 150+ individual study arms
These performance improvements are from the official ISSN position stand, not alkaline water studies specifically. They establish that the bicarbonate mechanism is real and dose-dependent. Alkaline water delivers far less bicarbonate per liter than these supplement doses, but it uses the identical mechanism.
Alkaline water vs. baking soda: the dose gap
One liter of alkaline water at pH 9.0 contains roughly 1-2 mmol of bicarbonate. The ISSN ergogenic dose (0.3 g/kg for a 70kg person = 21g of NaHCO3) contains about 250 mmol. So alkaline water is roughly 100-200x lower dose than ergogenic supplementation per liter. This is why you need 2.5-3 liters daily for the combat sports study to show effects. The mechanism is the same. The dose requires volume.
Why athletes use baking soda, not alkaline water
Baking soda is cheaper, more concentrated, and dose-controllable. Alkaline water has a practical advantage in combat sports specifically: athletes often restrict water intake for weight cuts before competition. Switching to alkaline water instead of plain water during the rehydration window adds bicarbonate load without requiring a separate supplement protocol. It combines hydration and buffering in one intervention.
03
The Combat Sports RCT: The Best Direct Evidence for Alkaline Water
Chycki et al. PLoS ONE 2018 · n=16 · double-blind placebo-controlled · 3-week protocol · Wingate test performance

This is the most directly relevant study for your argument. Double-blind, placebo-controlled, real athletes, real performance endpoints. Not a supplement study — alkaline water itself vs. regular water, over three weeks at realistic daily intake volumes.

Alkaline Water in Combat Sport Athletes: Acid-Base and Performance Results
Chycki et al. PLoS ONE 2018 · n=16 well-trained males · 7.6 years combat sport experience · double-blind placebo RCT · 3 weeks · 2.5-3L/day
All four key outcomes moved in the expected direction in the alkaline water group. The study was double-blind and placebo-controlled, which is a high bar for a water study. The 0.71-second improvement in Wingate performance is small in absolute terms but meaningful in competitive combat sports where margins are tight.
Post-Exercise Rehydration: Blood Viscosity After Alkaline vs. Regular Water
Weidman et al. J Int Soc Sports Nutr 2016 · n=100 · randomized double-blind · post-exercise dehydration rehydration protocol · p=0.03
This was a 100-person double-blind RCT, the largest controlled study on alkaline water. Plasma osmolality, bioimpedance, and body mass showed no significant difference between groups — so alkaline water did not hydrate "more" in those measures. The blood viscosity finding is the notable signal: faster-flowing blood after exercise could meaningfully impact recovery and next-bout performance.
04
GERD and Reflux: The Most Underreported Application
Koufman and Johnston Annals Otol Rhinol Laryngol 2012 · Hohenester et al. BMJ Open Gastroenterol 2023 · Zalvan et al. LPR alkaline diet study 2017

For GERD and especially laryngopharyngeal reflux (LPR), the evidence is more developed than most people realize. The mechanism here is not about buffering the buffering system — it is about directly deactivating the enzyme responsible for tissue damage in the esophagus and throat.

Why pH 8.8 Alkaline Water Is Different for Reflux Patients
Koufman and Johnston Annals Otol Rhinol Laryngol 2012 · in vitro pepsin denaturation study · pH 8.8 artesian well water · Hohenester BMJ Open Gastroenterol 2023
LPR: The stronger application
Laryngopharyngeal reflux is reflux that reaches the throat, vocal cords, and sinuses. It often occurs without classic heartburn. The Zalvan et al. study found that alkaline water combined with a Mediterranean-style diet achieved symptom control comparable to PPI medication over 6 weeks, avoiding the drug interaction and long-term PPI risks. For LPR specifically, the pepsin denaturation mechanism is particularly relevant because pepsin survives and stays active in the throat even after acid is gone.
The important caveat for GERD
These studies use bicarbonate-rich mineral water, not all alkaline water products. Electrolyzed alkaline water (the kind most brands sell) achieves high pH differently and may not deliver the same mineral content. The pepsin denaturation finding (Koufman 2012) is in vitro only. The 2023 RCT is the most clinically meaningful piece of evidence, and it specifically used bicarbonate-rich water. Brand matters here more than pH alone.
05
Diet Acid Load: Why Fruits, Vegetables, and Keto All Matter Here
PRAL (Potential Renal Acid Load) system · dietary acid load research · metabolic acidosis and protein synthesis · Thomas et al. 2023

The buffering system cost argument applies most strongly to people whose diet is producing the highest acid load — and the two clearest examples are chronic keto dieters and people who eat very little fruit and vegetables.

Dietary Acid Load by Food Type: Potential Renal Acid Load (PRAL)
PRAL system (Remer and Manz) · negative PRAL = alkaline-forming · positive PRAL = acid-forming · values per 100g typical serving
Why keto dieters have different pH balance
Ketone bodies (beta-hydroxybutyrate, acetoacetate) are weak acids. Combined with the high protein intake typical of keto, the total renal acid load is higher than most other diets. Long-term keto dieters measurably have lower urine pH because their kidneys are excreting more hydrogen ions. This is not dangerous in healthy people with normal renal function, but it represents exactly the buffering cost scenario where alkaline water would have the most measurable effect.
Diet is the better solution than bottled water
Fruits and vegetables have strongly negative PRAL values, meaning they actively reduce the kidney's acid excretion burden. A diet adequate in plant foods essentially does what alkaline water proponents claim for the supplement. This is why your Reddit comment was correct: for healthy non-athletes eating adequate fruit and vegetables, alkaline water adds little because the diet is already reducing acid load. Baking soda costs pennies and delivers the same bicarbonate at far higher doses.
06
Claim by Claim: What the Evidence Actually Supports
Alkaline water is a complete scam
Overreach
There are double-blind RCTs showing benefits in combat sport athletes, post-exercise rehydration, and GERD patients. Calling it a scam ignores real primary literature. The marketing is often scammy. The mechanism is real. Those are different things.
Alkaline water changes your blood pH
Not meaningfully — but that misses the point
Blood pH stays within its 7.35-7.45 range due to the buffering system. But the buffering system doing that work has a metabolic cost. Reducing the incoming acid load reduces the cost of maintaining pH, which matters most during high-intensity exercise. Urine pH does measurably change.
High-intensity anaerobic athletes benefit from alkaline water
Supported by RCT data
Chycki et al. 2018 (double-blind, placebo-controlled, n=16 combat sport athletes): improved blood and urine pH, lower post-exercise lactate, +0.71s Wingate improvement (p=0.03) after 3 weeks at 2.5-3L/day. Consistent with the established sodium bicarbonate ergogenic mechanism.
Alkaline water helps with GERD or LPR
Emerging evidence — most promising for LPR
pH 8.8 water permanently denatures pepsin in vitro (Koufman 2012). 2023 RCT in GERD patients found reduced heartburn with bicarbonate-rich water vs. regular water. LPR study found alkaline water plus Mediterranean diet achieved symptom control comparable to PPIs. Best evidence is for bicarbonate-rich mineral water specifically.
Alkaline water prevents cancer, slows aging, or detoxifies
Not supported
No clinical evidence supports any of these claims. Blood pH cannot be meaningfully altered by drinking water. In vitro cancer cell studies showing alkaline conditions kill cancer cells do not translate to human physiology. This is where the scam label is deserved.
You need to buy alkaline water to get these benefits
No — cheaper alternatives exist
Baking soda (sodium bicarbonate) costs pennies, delivers 100-200x more bicarbonate per dose, and has an ISSN position stand. A diet rich in fruits and vegetables reduces dietary acid load through PRAL. For GERD, bicarbonate-rich mineral water (not necessarily electrolyzed alkaline water) is what the research actually tested.
Synthesis: Who Should Actually Care About This
The alkaline water debate has two equally wrong camps. The marketers oversell it for cancer prevention, longevity, and universal health optimization. Their critics dismiss it entirely based on a correct but incomplete understanding of blood pH regulation. Both sides are making the same mistake: treating it as a binary question when the evidence is clearly population-specific.

For high-intensity anaerobic athletes — especially combat sport athletes, swimmers, rowers, and cyclists doing efforts between 30 seconds and 12 minutes — the bicarbonate mechanism is real, validated by the ISSN, and directly applicable. Alkaline water can serve as a lower-concentration, hydration-combined version of the baking soda supplementation protocol that elite athletes already use. The dosage requirement (2.5-3 liters daily for weeks) is higher than a baking soda pill, but for athletes already tracking hydration, it is practical.

For GERD and LPR patients, the pepsin denaturation mechanism at pH 8.8 is the strongest science-based argument for alkaline water specifically. Pepsin, not just acid, causes tissue damage in reflux, and PPIs do not address it. The 2023 RCT and the LPR study both support bicarbonate-rich water as a meaningful adjunct. This is probably the most actionable application for non-athletes.

For the average healthy adult eating adequate fruits and vegetables, doing moderate exercise, and without significant GERD: the honest answer is that alkaline water will likely do nothing a glass of regular water would not. The diet-driven reduction in acid load from plants does most of what alkaline water proponents claim, and baking soda covers the rest for a fraction of the price.

The research is expanding. What was fringe is becoming mainstream, particularly in sports science. The scam label belongs on the marketing, not on the mechanism.
